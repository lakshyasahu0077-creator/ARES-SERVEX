1. Scope and Authority

The ARES-SURVEX Planetary Cartography & Surface Survival System is an authorized aerospace guidance tool governed by the Interplanetary Exploration Directorate (IED), the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA), and cooperating international space agencies. By initiating an Extravehicular Activity (EVA) route or logging planetary specimens, crew members and flight controllers agree to comply with the stipulations set forth herein.

2. Flight Rule Adherence & Consumables Thresholds

All planned traverses must conform to Flight Rule EVA-704 regarding Primary Life Support System (PLSS) consumable margins:

  • Minimum O2 Reserve: No EVA profile shall be approved if projected return-to-base (RTB) oxygen reserves fall below 180 liters (25% nominal tank margin).
  • Battery Energy Reserve: Traverses must guarantee a minimum 20% remaining battery capacity upon re-pressurization in the habitat airlock.
  • Return-to-Base (RTB) Absolute Rule: Upon issuance of an automated CRITICAL_ABORT or IMMEDIATE_RTB warning by the ARES-SURVEX engine, the EVA team shall immediately cease scientific sampling and proceed along the calculated reciprocal emergency azimuth back to base camp.

3. COSPAR Planetary Protection Compliance (Category IV-b)

Martian surface operations fall under strict Committee on Space Research (COSPAR) Planetary Protection Category IV-b directives. Traverses entering designated "Special Regions" (defined as areas where terrestrial organisms might propagate, including recurring slope lineae and subsurface ice scarps) require prior clearance from the Mission Planetary Protection Officer. Unauthorized breach of biological containment envelopes carries severe disciplinary and operational penalties.

4. Terrain Hazards & Slope Grade Restrictions

Traversing slopes exceeding 25.0 degrees or entering uncharacterized soft regolith fields (shear strength < 2.5 kPa) is strictly prohibited without tethering and ground-radar subsurface sounding. The ARES-SURVEX automated hazard boundary calculations serve as primary navigational advisories; however, on-site astronaut visual confirmation remains mandatory.

5. Sample Custody & Chain of Evidence

Every regolith core or rock fragment logged into the Science Sample Registry must be sealed within an approved titanium-nitride hermetic sample tube. Falsification of geochemical coordinates, unauthorized sample hoarding, or breach of tamper-evident seals violates planetary science treaties.

6. Limitation of Liability in Deep Space Operations

ARES-SURVEX provides real-time algorithmic trajectory optimization and sensor synthesis. Unforeseen rapid Martian meteorological events, such as planet-encircling dust storms (tau > 3.0), solar particle events (SPE), or catastrophic equipment fractures, remain inherent operational hazards of the Martian environment.
